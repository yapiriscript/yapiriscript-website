Section 01

Introduction

Yapiri is learned as a sound-to-symbol system: hear or say a sound, choose its character, and write the sequence in the order it is spoken.

How to use this page

Read the terms first, keep the alphabet reference nearby, then work through the guided lessons. Use the Practice Lab for short drills and the Translation Exercise for longer writing.

One sound at a time

Vowels are full letters. Consonants keep their own sound values. Aspiration and tone carry meaning, so they should be written deliberately rather than guessed.

Static by design

The reference tables, lesson explanations, 500-word bank, practice instructions, and 1,000 sentence prompts are part of this HTML document. Scripts only add convenience.

Section 02

Terms to Know Before Learning

These eleven ideas appear throughout the lessons.

Vowel

A speech sound made with an open vocal tract. Yapiri writes each Kokborok vowel as a full letter.

Consonant

A speech sound made by narrowing or closing part of the vocal tract.

Diphthong

Two vowel qualities heard together within one syllable, such as ai.

Syllable

A beat of speech built around a vowel; it may begin or end with consonants.

Nasal

A consonant made while air passes through the nose, such as m, n, or ng.

Stop

A consonant made by briefly stopping the airflow, such as p, t, or k.

Aspirate

A stop released with an extra puff of air, as in ph, th, and kh.

Glide

A vowel-like consonant that moves quickly into a nearby vowel, such as y or w.

Tone

A contrast in pitch that can distinguish meaning. Yapiri marks high tone; low tone is unmarked.

Minimal Pair

Two forms that differ in only one sound or tone and therefore help train a precise contrast.

Punctuation

Marks that organize written sentences, including the comma, full stop, quotation mark, question mark, and exclamation mark.

Guided Lessons

Open the cards in order or use them independently. Progress is a small optional aid inside this section.

01 · Vowels

Goal

Recognise and write all six vowel letters: a, e, i, u, o, and ə/w. Each vowel is independent; none is hidden inside a consonant.

󱲠a
󱲡e
󱲢i
󱲣u
󱲤o
󱲥ə / w

Check

Point to each symbol, say its value, cover the labels, and repeat.

02 · Diphthongs

Goal

Read two written vowels as one syllable where Kokborok uses a diphthong. Start with ai and wi/ui, then practise oi, ua, and uo.

󱲠󱲢ai
󱲥󱲢wi
󱲤󱲢oi

Check

Clap once for the complete diphthong, not once for each written vowel.

03 · Consonants

Goal

Learn consonants by sound families: stops (p t k / b d g), aspirates (ph th kh), affricates (ch j), nasals (m n n′ ng), fricatives (s h), liquids (r l), glides (y w), and extended loan sounds (v z).

Check

Say each plain stop and its aspirated partner without adding an extra vowel.

04 · Syllables

Goal

Build syllables from a vowel nucleus: V (a), CV (ka), CVC (kok), and clusters where the language permits them. Read strictly left to right.

󱲩󱲠ka
󱲩󱲤󱲩kok
󱲪󱲤󱲷󱲤󱲩borok

Check

Underline the vowel in each syllable and identify any final consonant.

05 · Tone

Goal

Leave low tone unmarked. Place the high-tone combining mark after the vowel it modifies: 󱲩󱲠 ka versus 󱲩󱲠󱳑 ká.

Check

Make sure the mark visually attaches to the vowel rather than standing as punctuation.

06 · Punctuation

Goal

Use the Yapiri comma, full stop, exclamation mark, quotation mark, and question mark in the same structural positions as their Roman equivalents.

󱳋comma
󱳌full stop
󱳏question
07 · Numerals

Goal

Recognise Yapiri digits independently from letters. Copy 0–9, then write dates, prices, and short number sequences.

󱳀󱳁󱳂󱳃󱳄0–4
󱳅󱳆󱳇󱳈󱳉5–9
08 · Minimal Pairs

Goal

Train one contrast at a time: p/ph, t/th, k/kh, n/ng, and low/high tone. A minimal-pair drill is about precision, not speed.

󱲧 / 󱲭p / ph
󱲨 / 󱲮t / th
󱲩 / 󱲯k / kh
