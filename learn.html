Lesson 01 · Orientation

Start Here: How Yapiri Works

Yapiri is a phonemic script: each core character points to a sound in Kokborok. Start by noticing that the script is learned through sound values first, then words, then sentences.

Focus 1

Characters represent sounds, not English letters.

Focus 2

Roman Kokborok helps you check pronunciation while learning.

Focus 3

The same symbols will reappear in the keyboard and translation exercises.

Lesson 02 · Core sounds

Vowels First

Learn the six vowel characters first: /a e ə i o u/. The schwa /ə/ is a real Kokborok vowel here, not a weak filler sound, so treat it as its own character.

Focus 1

Read every vowel as a full sound.

Focus 2

Keep a and ə separate.

Focus 3

Use vowel pairs to train your eye before adding consonants.

Lesson 04 · Contrast

Plain and Aspirated Stops

Kokborok distinguishes plain and aspirated stops. The pairs k/kh, t/th, and p/ph are separate sound values, so do not treat the h as decoration.

Focus 1

Plain and aspirated stops are different phonemes.

Focus 2

k, t, p are not the same as kh, th, ph.

Focus 3

Train the pairs side by side before reading longer words.

Lesson 06 · Sound families

Nasals and Close Sounds

Kokborok uses several nasal sounds. Pay special attention to n, ng, and n‑prime because they look and behave as separate writing choices.

Focus 1

m, n, and ng are separate characters.

Focus 2

ng is common and should be read as one sound.

Focus 3

n‑prime is used in specific conventional forms such as in’ and in’he.

Lesson 08 · Meaning detail

Tone Mark

The high tone mark is a combining sign. It attaches above a base character and should be learned separately from ordinary punctuation.

Focus 1

Tone is written with a combining mark.

Focus 2

The mark belongs above a base character.

Focus 3

Tone can distinguish meaning, so it is part of reading, not decoration.
