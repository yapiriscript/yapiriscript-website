How to Use

Tips & Notes

01
Schwa vs. Consonant W

These two characters serve distinct positional roles.

The consonant W () only appears word-initially... The schwa ə () only appears mid-word...

Consonant W — word start
 wak — pig  ·  w · a · k
Schwa ə — mid-word
 kwtwi — sweet  ·  k · ə · t · ə · i
02
Diacritic Usage

Yapiri has two combining diacritics, each with a specific rule.

High Tone Mark (◌́) — over the vowel ́ bohrok — they  ·  b · ó · r · o · k
Reduplication — within a word, no hyphen
̋ nini — yours  ·  n · i̋ (mark signals ni repeats)
Reduplication — consecutive words, add hyphen
̋- serek serek — silently  ·  s · e · r · e · k̋ · hyphen
03
Extended & Secondary Keys

The gold-bordered keys are outside the core native Kokborok alphabet — each has a specific classification:

v, z — secondary loanword characters. Use for borrowed vocabulary and scientific names where no native equivalent exists.

f — optional extended character. In native Kokborok text, ph is the standard equivalent. Use f only when you wish to explicitly distinguish it from ph in loanword writing.

chh — secondary, pending linguistic documentation. Formally attested in phonological descriptions of Kokborok, but no active lexical examples confirmed. Available for specialist use.
