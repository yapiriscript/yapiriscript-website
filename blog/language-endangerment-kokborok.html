Language Endangerment and What It Means for Kokborok

When a language fades, an entire way of seeing, feeling, and understanding the world disappears with it. For the Tiprasa people, this is not a distant threat — it is a living reality we must face together.

May 2026 13 min read Animesh Debbarma

Every time a language dies, humanity loses a unique library of knowledge, stories, humour, and wisdom that took thousands of years to evolve. Kokborok, the heartbeat of the Tiprasa people, stands today at a quiet but real crossroads.

This is not just about words. It is about identity, memory, and the very soul of our community.

What Language Endangerment Really Means

UNESCO measures the health of a language largely by one question: is it still being passed to children as a mother tongue? When that transmission slows, a language begins to slide along a scale — from safe, to vulnerable, to endangered, and eventually to extinct. The domains in which it is used start to shrink: from offices to homes, from everyday speech to only songs and ceremonies.

When a language weakens, it is not merely vocabulary that is lost. Entire systems of thought, relationships with the natural world, traditional medicine, spiritual concepts, and humour rooted in cultural context begin to fade with it.

"A language is not just a tool for communication. It is a way of being in the world."

— A common reflection among linguists

Where Kokborok Stands Today

Kokborok is not extinct, and it is not yet endangered. It sits at a stage that should make us pay attention: the UNESCO Atlas of the World's Languages in Danger classifies Kokborok as Vulnerable.

UNESCO Language Vitality Scale — where Kokborok sits
SafeSpoken by all generations; transmission unbroken
VulnerableMost children speak it, but use is restricted to certain domains, such as the home — Kokborok is here
Definitely endangeredChildren no longer learn it as a mother tongue
Severely endangeredSpoken only by grandparents and older generations
Critically endangeredSpoken partially and infrequently by the oldest few

"Vulnerable" means a language still has children speaking it, but its use is being squeezed into narrower and narrower corners of life. It is the gentlest warning on the scale — and the easiest stage from which to recover, if we act while the warning is still gentle.

The Pressures on Kokborok

Several forces are pressing on the language at once: urbanisation, migration for education and jobs, a preference for dominant languages in schools, and the long historical dominance of Bengali in administration and media. In Tripura itself, census figures tell the story plainly — only around a quarter of the state's population speaks Kokborok, while roughly two-thirds speak Bengali. For an official language of the state, that is a sobering imbalance.

Reality Many Tiprasa children today understand Kokborok but cannot speak it fluently. Others can speak but cannot read or write it confidently. This gradual shift, if left unchanged, is exactly how a vulnerable language slips toward endangerment over two or three generations.

What We Stand to Lose

The weakening of Kokborok would mean losing far more than a vocabulary:

  • Traditional ecological knowledge embedded in the words themselves
  • Unique expressions of love, respect, grief, and joy
  • Oral histories, folk tales, and proverbs that define the Tiprasa worldview
  • The ability to fully express our cultural and spiritual identity
  • A direct, unbroken connection to our ancestors

Language is the DNA of culture. When it weakens, our distinctiveness as a people slowly dissolves into the mainstream.

Signs of Hope and Revival

The story does not have to end in loss. Around the world, vulnerable and endangered languages are being revived through community effort, documentation, literature, music, and — crucially — writing systems built for the language itself. The fact that Kokborok is still only "vulnerable" means the window for action is wide open.

For Kokborok, the emergence of indigenous scripts — from Aima, Kokmari, Hachukma, and now Yapiri — represents a powerful act of cultural self-determination. Writing our language with dignity and accuracy is one of the strongest tools we have against forgetting.

The Role of Yapiri

Yapiri was created with this reality in mind. By designing a script that fits Kokborok's phonology precisely — without forcing it into a borrowed structure — we lower the barriers to learning and using the language in written form. The thinking behind that design is explained in why Yapiri is a phonemic alphabet.

A script that feels natural and beautiful encourages more people to read, write, create, and share in Kokborok. It gives young people pride in their linguistic identity — and pride is one of the quiet engines of revival.

What Each of Us Can Do

Language revitalisation is not the job of linguists or activists alone. It begins at home:

  • Speak Kokborok with your children, every day
  • Sing lullabies and folk songs in the language
  • Write letters, poems, and messages in Kokborok
  • Support creators who produce content in Kokborok
  • Learn and use a script that honours the language — you can start on the web keyboard

Every word spoken, every line written, is a small act of resistance against forgetting.

The Heart of the Matter

Kokborok is not just a language. It is the voice of our ancestors, the laughter of our grandmothers, the wisdom of our land, and the hope of our children. Its survival is tied to the survival of our identity as Tiprasa people.

We stand at a turning point — and "vulnerable" is the stage at which turning points still favour us. The decisions we make today, in our homes, schools, and creative spaces, will determine whether Kokborok continues as a living, thriving language or becomes a memory in books.

Let us choose to write, speak, sing, and dream in Kokborok — so that future generations may know who they are, where they come from, and the beauty of the language that carries their soul. If you would like to be part of that effort, the Community page is where it gathers.
