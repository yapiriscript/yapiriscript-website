Yapiri Script v1.0 — A Writing System Born From the Tiprasa People

After months of design, engineering, and community feedback, the first public version of the Yapiri script is here. This post traces the journey from hand-drawn glyphs to a fully encoded OpenType font — and what comes next.

Every writing system begins with a question: what sounds need to be captured, and how? For Kokborok — a Tibeto-Burman language spoken by over a million Tiprasa people across Tripura — the existing answers never quite fit. Bengali script was borrowed. Roman script was adapted. Neither was designed from within the language itself.

Yapiri was created to change that. Not as a romanization, not as an adaptation — but as a phonemic alphabet built entirely around the sounds of Kokborok, designed from first principles by a member of the Tiprasa community.

From Sketches to Glyphs

The process began with phonological analysis — mapping every sound in Kokborok and deciding which needed its own dedicated symbol. The inventory that emerged: 25 consonants, 6 vowels, 10 numerals, 5 punctuation marks, and two combining diacritics for tone and reduplication.

Each glyph was drawn by hand in Inkscape using Bézier curves, with rounded linecaps and consistent stroke weight throughout. The design rule was strict: every character had to feel like part of one visual family, not a collection of individually designed symbols.

Aspirated consonants are derived from their plain counterparts through a consistent visual modification — so a learner who knows /p/ recognises /ph/ by pattern. Voiced stops share structural DNA with their voiceless pairs. The script encodes phonological relationships directly into its visual system.

Engineering the Font

The glyphs were imported into FontForge where the real engineering began. PUA codepoints were assigned across the range U+E000 – U+E02D. OpenType GPOS mark-to-base anchors were implemented for diacritic positioning. Advance widths were calibrated. The font was exported as OTF, TTF, and WOFF2.

The final font ships with full Unicode PUA encoding, OpenType GPOS support, and consistent metrics across all 48 characters. It renders correctly in web browsers, word processors, and design applications.

What Comes Next

Version 1.0 is a foundation, not a finish line. The roadmap ahead includes a Keyman keyboard package for native system input, a formal Unicode submission through the Script Encoding Initiative at UC Berkeley, and — most importantly — community adoption across Tripura and the Tiprasa diaspora.

If you speak, teach, write, or love Kokborok, this script belongs to you. Download the font, try the keyboard tool, share it with your community. Every use is a footprint — and footprints are what Yapiri is named for.
